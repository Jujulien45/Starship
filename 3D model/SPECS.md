
CanSat requirements

Summary of the technical requirements for your CanSat. The official guidelines (FR/NL) remain the reference document.
1. Size

All the components of the CanSat must fit inside a standard soft drinks can (115 mm height and 66 mm diameter), with the exception of the parachute. Antennas can be mounted externally on the top or bottom of the can, depending on the design, but not on the sides.

Note: the rocket payload has 4.5 cm of space per CanSat available along the can's axial dimension (i.e. height), which must accommodate all external elements, including the parachute, parachute attachment hardware and any antennas.
2. Additional components

The antennas, transducers and other elements of the CanSat cannot extend beyond the can's diameter until it has left the rocket.
3. Mass

The mass of the CanSat must be between 300 g and 350 g. CanSats that are lighter must take additional ballast with them to reach the 300 g minimum.
4. Forbidden materials

Explosives, detonators, pyrotechnics, and inflammable or dangerous materials are strictly forbidden. All materials used must be safe for the personnel, the equipment, and the environment.
5. Power

The CanSat must be powered by a battery and/or solar panels. The systems must be able to remain switched on for four continuous hours.
6. Battery accessibility

The battery must be easily accessible in case it has to be replaced or recharged.
7. Power switch

The CanSat must have an easily accessible master power switch.
8. GPS tracker

During the preparation day, each team will receive a GPS tracker (SafeTag Mini 4G) to track the CanSat after the flight. The tracker measures 35 x 20 x 70 mm and weighs 70 g. The GPS data transmission and reception is handled by the rocket provider, so you do not have to worry about this.

The tracker is generally attached to the outside of the can. It is not part of the size requirement, but you have to take it into account during the design phase.

The tracker's weight is not part of the mass requirement (300-350 g), but you must include it when designing the parachute: CanSat + tracker = 370-420 g.

If your secondary mission prevents the use of the tracker, you must explain why and incorporate an appropriate recovery system.
9. Safe landing system

The CanSat should have a reusable safe landing system, such as a parachute. Brightly coloured fabric is recommended, as it makes recovery after landing easier.
10. Parachute strength

The parachute connection must be able to withstand up to 50 N of force. The strength of the parachute must be tested to ensure that the system will operate nominally.
11. Maximum flight time

For recovery reasons, a maximum flight time of 120 seconds is recommended for a launch height of 1000 m. If attempting a directed landing, a maximum of 170 seconds is recommended.
12. Descent rate

A descent rate between 8 and 11 m/s is recommended for recovery reasons. For safety reasons, the descent rate must never be lower than 5 m/s or higher than 12 m/s.
13. Acceleration

The CanSat must be able to withstand an acceleration of up to 20 g.
